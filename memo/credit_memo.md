# Credit Memo: Bunge Global SA (BG)

**To:** Credit Committee | **From:** Ashkan Loghmani | **Date:** November 2026
**Request:** Approve a $500 million, 3-year senior unsecured revolving credit commitment to Bunge Global SA, for general corporate and working capital purposes (illustrative request for this exercise).

## Recommendation

**Approve, with conditions.** Bunge's leverage more than doubled in 2025 (Debt/EBITDA 3.1x → 7.7x reported, about 5.7x adjusted). The cause was the debt-funded Viterra acquisition, not weaker operations. Operating earnings before interest were flat year on year. Bunge has $10.2 billion of liquidity and no rating triggers, and much of its balance sheet is commodity inventory that can be sold quickly. So the risk is acceptable for a short-dated revolver, provided Bunge shows it can bring leverage back down. The conditions are listed under Mitigants and conditions below.

## Borrower

Bunge Global SA (NYSE: BG) is one of the world's largest agribusiness and food companies. Founded in Amsterdam in 1818, it is now incorporated in Switzerland and run from St. Louis, Missouri. Its main business is buying, storing, transporting and processing oilseeds and grains such as soybeans, canola and wheat into vegetable oils, protein meals for animal feed, and ingredients for food and biofuel makers. In 2025 it completed its merger with Viterra, which made its global grain origination and trading network much larger.

**Why Bunge and not General Mills:** General Mills ranks higher on the screen (9.4x Debt/EBITDA), but most of its earnings decline comes from $2.9B of restructuring and impairment charges. Its operating cash flow ($2.2B) exceeded its reported EBITDA, and adjusted for those charges its leverage is about 3.1x. Bunge's leverage comes from new debt, not an accounting charge, so it is the weaker credit.

## What's driving it

- **The Viterra acquisition was paid for with debt.** The deal closed on July 2, 2025, on the following terms:
  - Viterra shareholders received approximately 65.6 million shares of Bunge, with an aggregate value of approximately $5.3 billion, and $1.9 billion in cash in return for 100% of the outstanding equity of Viterra.
  - Bunge also repaid $3.6 billion of Viterra's debt at closing. Total consideration was $10.6 billion.
  - Bunge assumed a further $4.6 billion of Viterra debt: $1.1 billion short-term, $1.2 billion current long-term and $2.2 billion long-term.
- **Debt rose by $7.8 billion, from $6.2 billion to $14.1 billion.** About $4.6 billion was debt assumed from Viterra. Most of the rest was new borrowing to pay the cash part of the deal:
  - $1.3 billion of new senior notes
  - short-term debt up from $0.9 billion to $3.9 billion
- **87% of the leverage increase comes from debt and 13% from lower EBITDA.**
  - Holding 2024 EBITDA constant, the new debt alone takes Debt/EBITDA from 3.1x to 7.1x.
  - The drop in EBITDA from $1,992 million to $1,837 million adds the last 0.6x.
- **Margins fell because revenue grew but profit did not.**
  - Revenue rose 32% to $70.3 billion, but gross profit was flat ($3,393M → $3,409M). Gross margin fell from 6.4% to 4.8%.
  - Viterra's grain trading business has high volume and thin margins.
  - Costs rose at the same time: SG&A +$337M, depreciation +$235M (the larger asset base) and interest +$157M.
  - As a result, pre-tax income fell 26% to $1,134 million.
- **Cash flow did not cover cash spending.**
  - Operating cash flow fell from $1.9 billion to $844 million, which covers only 6% of debt. Rising inventories (+$700M) and receivables (+$469M) took up cash.
  - Against that, Bunge spent:
    - $4.2 billion on acquisitions (net of cash acquired)
    - $1.7 billion on capex
    - $1.0 billion on buybacks and dividends
  - The gap was funded by new debt and by running cash down from $3.3 billion to $1.1 billion.
- **Judgement: the leverage is partly temporary, but it will not fix itself.**
  - Part is temporary. The 2025 results include only six months of Viterra while the debt is counted in full. Working capital should also release cash once the integration settles.
  - But Bunge's own pro forma figures, which treat Viterra as owned for all of 2025, show net income of $835 million, against $816 million reported. A full year of Viterra therefore adds little profit.
  - Leverage will come down only if Bunge delivers merger synergies and uses its cash to repay debt instead of funding buybacks. This is why the recommendation carries conditions.

## Risks a lender would worry about

- **Integration risk.** Viterra roughly doubled the grain network. If cost savings come late or the systems integration goes badly, EBITDA stays near $2.5 billion (adjusted) and leverage stays above 5x.
- **Refinancing and short-term funding.** $3.9 billion of short-term debt and $1.4 billion of long-term debt fall due in 2026, and another $1.4 billion in 2027. Bunge relies on commercial paper and bank lines rolling over. If markets tighten or its rating is cut, its cost of funds rises when coverage is already thin (2.8x adjusted).
- **Commodity price swings.** Higher grain and oilseed prices increase the cash tied up in inventory and margin calls, so borrowing rises exactly when markets are volatile. A sharp drop in crush margins hits EBITDA directly.
- **Weather and harvests.** Droughts or floods in the U.S., Brazil, Argentina or Canada cut the volumes Bunge handles and can leave its processing plants underused, reducing cash flow available for debt service.
- **Trade and tariffs.** U.S.-China trade disputes shift soybean flows between the U.S. and South America. That can strand assets in one region and make earnings harder to predict.
- **Geopolitics and energy.** The Russia-Ukraine war disrupts Black Sea grain flows, and conflict in the Middle East raises energy and freight costs, which squeezes processing margins.
- **Working-capital intensity.** The business needs large, swinging amounts of cash to buy and hold crops. Operating cash flow can fall sharply in a single year, as it did in 2025 ($1.9B → $0.8B), even when earnings are stable.
- **Shareholder returns ahead of lenders.** Bunge spent $1.0 billion on buybacks and dividends in 2025 while borrowing to close the deal. If this continues, debt reduction will be slower.

## Mitigants and conditions

**Mitigants**

- **Strong liquidity.** Bunge has $1.1 billion of cash and $9.1 billion of unused committed bank facilities, about $10.2 billion in total. That is roughly twice the 2026 short-term and long-term maturities combined.
- **Liquid assets.** Inventories are $13.2 billion, much of it exchange-traded grains and oilseeds that can be sold quickly. That gives lenders a cushion that a branded food company does not have.
- **No rating triggers.** Bunge's debt agreements do not have any credit rating downgrade triggers that would accelerate repayment. Bunge has stated an objective of keeping an investment-grade rating.
- **Stable operating earnings.** Adjusted EBITDA held at about $2.46 billion through the deal year. The business generated cash before interest even as margins fell.
- **Long-dated debt.** About a third of long-term debt ($3.4 billion of $10.4 billion) matures after 2030.

**Conditions**

1. A maximum leverage covenant of 4.5x adjusted Debt/EBITDA, tested quarterly, stepping down to 3.5x by December 31, 2027.
2. A minimum interest coverage covenant of 2.5x (adjusted EBIT / interest).
3. Quarterly financial statements and a compliance certificate, plus a progress update on Viterra synergies.
4. A full credit review in 12 months, or earlier if Bunge loses its investment-grade rating.
5. Pricing tied to Bunge's credit rating, so the margin rises automatically if the rating falls.
